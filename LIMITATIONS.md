# POLARIS-AI Known Limitations and Operational Boundaries

**System:** POLARIS-AI Antarctic Decision Support System  
**Audience:** Bridge Navigation Officers, Ice Navigators, and Logistics Planners  

---

## 1. Remote Sensing Sensor Boundaries

### 1. Passive Microwave Resolution Limits
- Satellite sea-ice concentration products derived from DMSP SSMIS and AMSR2 (NOAA/NSIDC G02135) operate at a nominal grid cell resolution of **25 km**.
- Coastal polynyas, leads, and shore cracks smaller than 25 km cannot be resolved in gridded passive microwave products. Bridge officers must cross-reference high-resolution Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR, e.g. Sentinel-1 50m resolution) imagery when operating in close proximity to Antarctic ice tongues.

### 2. Summer Surface Melt Pond Bias
- During the austral summer (December to February), solar radiation creates surface melt ponds on sea-ice floes. Passive microwave brightness temperatures can misinterpret wet ice surfaces and melt ponds as open water, leading to a local underestimation of actual floe coverage by 5% to 15%.

### 3. Polar Hole Satellite Data Void
- Due to satellite orbital inclination, passive microwave sensors have a polar observation void centered on the South Pole. This region is masked as permanent continental ice.

## 2. Dynamic Iceberg Drift Boundaries

### 1. Calving Event Unpredictability
- Hydrodynamic momentum equations project the future trajectory of existing, tracked icebergs whose mass and initial coordinates are recorded. They cannot predict catastrophic calving of new tabular icebergs from parent ice shelves.

### 2. Deep Keel Grounding on Seamounts
- Tabular icebergs with drafts of 200–300 m can ground on uncharted banks and shallow bathymetric rises (e.g. around South Georgia, Maud Rise, or the Amery bank). The current 2D momentum formulation does not incorporate fine-scale 3D bathymetric bottom friction.

### 3. Underwater Wave Erosion and Cleaving
- Wave-induced mechanical cleavage, thermal melting, and capsize dynamics are modeled through ensemble variance envelopes ($p_{10}, p_{50}, p_{90}$) rather than explicit 3D structural fracture mechanics.

## 3. Route Optimization and Physics Boundaries

### 1. Advisory Decision Support Status
- Routes generated by POLARIS-AI are advisory recommendations intended to augment, not supersede, the judgment of certified Ice Navigators and Ship Masters under the IMO Polar Code and STCW Convention.

### 2. High-Latitude Geodesic Calculations
- Beyond 80°S, longitude meridians converge rapidly. While POLARIS-AI utilizes spherical Haversine and WGS84 geodesic calculations with antimeridian wrapping, standard Mercator bridge chart projections suffer from extreme scale distortion. Bridge displays must utilize Polar Stereographic or Gnomonic projections south of 60°S.

### 3. Fail-Safe Quality Gate
- If observational data latency exceeds **48 hours** or satellite concentration feeds drop out, the system automatically trips the fail-safe gate, transitioning into a `DO_NOT_USE_FOR_NAVIGATION` operational state (`fail_safe_gate_tripped = True`, `is_safe_for_decision_support = False`).

## 4. Machine Learning & Forecasting Model Boundaries

### 1. Pure ConvLSTM Spatial Diffusion
- Standalone recursive ConvLSTM rollouts over multi-day horizons exhibit numerical diffusion and spatial smoothing, leading the standalone network to underperform persistence on this polar grid (7-day mean RMSE: 0.0462 vs 0.0353).

### 2. Empirical Horizon Blending Schedule ($\alpha(\tau)$)
- Operational forecasting gains (+2.27% mean, +4.70% at Day 7) are achieved through a physics-guided hybrid combining kinematic wind advection, thermodynamic melt trends, and neural residual deltas via the horizon schedule $\alpha(\tau) = \min(0.35, 0.018 \cdot (\tau - 1)^{1.5})$.
- This schedule was empirically calibrated on the validation window (January 2026). While it encodes valid physical intuition (persistence dominates Day 1 while advection/thermodynamics govern extended horizons), validation across broader multi-seasonal test regimes is required prior to unmonitored operational deployment.
